### 1. Kanjivaram Silk (Kanchipuram)

**Origin:** Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu

**Characteristics:**
- Heavy, lustrous silk
- Distinctive temple borders
- Wide, elaborate pallus
- Pure gold and silver zari
- Weight: 600-900 grams

**Price range:** ₹3,000-30,000+ wholesale

**Best for:** Bridal, South Indian weddings, gift segment

**How to identify genuine:**
- GI tag
- Heavy feel
- Distinct korvai (border attached separately)
- Mulberry silk thread

---

### 2. Banarasi Silk

**Origin:** Varanasi (Banaras), Uttar Pradesh

**Characteristics:**
- Persian-influenced motifs
- Intricate zari work
- Mughal-era designs
- Heavier than other silks
- Distinctive brocade work

**Price range:** ₹2,000-20,000+ wholesale

**Best for:** North Indian weddings, Islamic aesthetic, traditional ceremonies

**Popular patterns:**
- Butidar (small motifs)
- Jangla (dense floral)
- Cutwork (sheer patterns)
- Tanchoi (single color with design)

---

### 3. Patola Silk

**Origin:** Patan, Gujarat

**Characteristics:**
- Double ikat weave (complex technique)
- Geometric patterns
- Takes months to weave
- Both sides identical
- Extremely rare

**Price range:** ₹5,000-1,00,000+ wholesale

**Best for:** Collectors, premium segment, discerning buyers

**Note:** Most "Patola" in market are printed imitations.

---

### 4. Tussar Silk (Kosa)

**Origin:** Multiple (Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha)

**Characteristics:**
- Wild silk (not mulberry)
- Natural golden/beige tone
- Rough, textured feel
- Lightweight
- Eco-friendly appeal

**Price range:** ₹1,500-6,000 wholesale

**Best for:** Eco-conscious buyers, contemporary styling, casual elegance

---

### 5. Mysore Silk

**Origin:** Karnataka (government-facilitated)

**Characteristics:**
- Lightweight compared to Kanjivaram
- Pure silk with zari
- Elegant, not heavy
- Distinctive government certification

**Price range:** ₹2,000-8,000 wholesale

**Best for:** South Indian daily elegance, office occasions

---

### 6. Chanderi Silk

**Origin:** Chanderi, Madhya Pradesh

**Characteristics:**
- Silk + cotton blend
- Very lightweight, sheer
- Delicate zari borders
- Elegant subtle appeal

**Price range:** ₹1,500-5,000 wholesale

**Best for:** Summer wear, light occasion wear, younger buyers

---

### 7. Bhagalpuri Silk (Tussar)

**Origin:** Bhagalpur, Bihar

**Characteristics:**
- Wild silk
- Simpler designs
- Affordable silk option
- Natural texture

**Price range:** ₹800-3,000 wholesale

**Best for:** Budget silk segment, modern designs

---

### 8. Muga Silk

**Origin:** Assam

**Characteristics:**
- Golden-yellow natural color
- Exclusive to Assam
- Extremely durable
- Rare and premium

**Price range:** ₹8,000-50,000+ wholesale

**Best for:** Collectors, NRI market, premium gifting

---

### Which Silk Types to Stock?

**For general retail:**
- 50% Banarasi and Kanjivaram (weddings)
- 30% Mysore and soft silk (occasions)
- 20% Tussar and Chanderi (contemporary)

**For premium boutique:**
- Higher proportion of Kanjivaram
- Some Patola for differentiation
- Muga for ultra-premium

**For online selling:**
- Focus on Banarasi (pan-India appeal)
- Soft silk (affordable silk)
- Avoid very heavy pieces (shipping cost)

---

### Identifying Genuine Silk

**Tests:**
1. Burn test – Real silk smells like burning hair
2. Weight – Pure silk is heavier
3. Lustre – Natural sheen, not plastic-like
4. Price – If too cheap, it's not pure silk

**Certifications:**
- Silk Mark (India)
- GI tags for regional varieties

## FAQs

### 1. Which is better, Kanjivaram or Banarasi?
Both are premium. Kanjivaram for South Indian taste, Banarasi for North Indian.

### 2. How to identify pure silk saree?
Burn test, weight, natural lustre, and Silk Mark certification.

### 3. Which silk saree is most expensive?
Patola (real double ikat) and Muga silk are most expensive.

### 4. What is soft silk saree?
Art silk or silk blend that resembles pure silk but at lower price.

### 5. Which silk saree sells most?
Banarasi for pan-India, Kanjivaram in South India.

### 6. Is Tussar silk pure silk?
Yes, it's wild silk (not mulberry) but 100% silk.
