Here are the major types:

---

### 1. Pure Handloom Cotton

**What it is:** Hand-woven on traditional looms.

**Characteristics:**
- Slightly uneven texture (natural)
- Breathable
- Long-lasting
- Develops softness with wash

**Price range:** ₹600-1,500 wholesale

**Best for:** Quality-conscious customers, hot climate regions.

---

### 2. Mill Cotton (Machine Woven)

**What it is:** Factory-produced cotton sarees.

**Characteristics:**
- Uniform weave
- Mass-produced
- Consistent quality
- Lower cost

**Price range:** ₹300-600 wholesale

**Best for:** Volume sales, budget customers.

---

### 3. Cotton Silk (Cotton + Silk Blend)

**What it is:** Cotton with silk yarn for shimmer.

**Characteristics:**
- Soft with subtle shine
- Looks premium
- Affordable alternative to pure silk

**Price range:** ₹500-1,200 wholesale

**Best for:** Office wear, semi-formal occasions.

---

### 4. Chanderi Cotton

**What it is:** From Chanderi, Madhya Pradesh.

**Characteristics:**
- Sheer, lightweight fabric
- Zari borders often
- Elegant drape
- Famous for butis (small motifs)

**Price range:** ₹800-2,500 wholesale

**Best for:** Festive occasions, boutique customers.

---

### 5. Mangalagiri Cotton

**What it is:** From Mangalagiri, Andhra Pradesh.

**Characteristics:**
- Distinct plain body with zari border
- Lightweight
- Excellent for South Indian market

**Price range:** ₹500-1,200 wholesale

**Best for:** South India retailers, daily elegance.

---

### 6. Kota Cotton (Kota Doria)

**What it is:** From Kota, Rajasthan.

**Characteristics:**
- Checkered texture (khats)
- Lightweight, transparent
- Perfect for summer

**Price range:** ₹600-1,500 wholesale

**Best for:** Summer collection, hot climates.

---

### 7. Tant Cotton

**What it is:** Bengal's traditional cotton weave.

**Characteristics:**
- Distinctive border patterns
- Lightweight
- Pastel and bright colors
- Daily wear staple in Bengal

**Price range:** ₹500-1,500 wholesale

**Best for:** East India market, modern daily wear.

---

### 8. Chettinad Cotton

**What it is:** From Chettinad, Tamil Nadu.

**Characteristics:**
- Bold stripes and checks
- Thick, durable cotton
- Bright colors

**Price range:** ₹500-1,000 wholesale

**Best for:** South India, statement daily wear.

---

### 9. Jamdani Cotton

**What it is:** Intricate woven patterns (originally Bangladesh).

**Characteristics:**
- Sheer fabric with opaque patterns
- Handwoven motifs
- Premium craftsmanship

**Price range:** ₹1,500-5,000 wholesale

**Best for:** Premium segment, art lovers.

---

### 10. Printed Cotton Sarees

**What it is:** Cotton with block, screen, or digital prints.

**Characteristics:**
- Wide variety of designs
- Trendy patterns
- Affordable
- Fast fashion

**Price range:** ₹300-700 wholesale

**Best for:** Volume sales, younger customers.

---

### Which Cotton Saree Types Sell Best?

Based on our 25+ years experience:

**Volume sellers:**
1. Mill cotton (budget segment)
2. Printed cotton (trend-driven)
3. Cotton silk (office wear)

**Premium sellers:**
1. Chanderi (festive)
2. Handloom (quality seekers)
3. Jamdani (art collectors)

**Regional preferences:**
- South India: Mangalagiri, Chettinad
- East India: Tant, Jamdani
- North India: Chanderi, Kota
- West India: Printed, mill cotton

**Recommended mix:**

## FAQs

### 1. Which cotton saree is best for daily wear?
Mill cotton and printed cotton for budget-friendly daily wear. Cotton silk for office elegance.

### 2. What is the most expensive cotton saree type?
Jamdani and fine handloom varieties command highest prices.

### 3. Which cotton saree is best for summer?
Kota cotton, Chanderi, and any lightweight handloom for breathability.

### 4. What is cotton silk saree?
A blend of cotton and silk yarns, offering comfort with subtle sheen.

### 5. Is handloom cotton better than mill cotton?
Handloom is more breathable and durable but more expensive.

### 6. Which cotton saree sells most in South India?
Mangalagiri, Chettinad, and soft cotton silk varieties.
